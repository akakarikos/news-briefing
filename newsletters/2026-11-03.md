## 1. European regulation & supervision

**ECB deadline for AI-cyber action plans passed (31 Oct)** (31 Oct). Following the Supervisory Board chair's July letter, every SSM significant institution had to give its JST a plan covering attack-surface inventory, faster patching at scale, AI-enabled detection with human oversight, supply-chain assurance and, in the medium term, legacy replacement. The ECB will now compare all plans and share conclusions. The annual IT Risk Questionnaire moves from September 2026 to February 2027, and a separate post-quantum communication is promised. ([A&O Shearman analysis](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ecb-requires-significant-institutions-to-address-ai-enabled-cybersecurity-threats))

*Why it matters for us:* The plan is now a supervisory commitment. Its milestones (exposed surface, IBM i and TIBCO patch cadence, legacy decommissioning) should be traceable in the SAB roadmap, because the JST will check delivery against them.

**AMLA central-database RTS consultation closes (3 Nov)** (3 Nov). The draft standard defines what supervisors will feed into AMLA's AML/CFT central database: risk profiles, sanctions and measures, authorisations, and fit-and-proper outcomes, with data-quality and confidentiality rules. A public hearing was held the same afternoon. ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/10/amla-consults-on-draft-rts-for-aml-cft-central-database/))

*Why it matters for us:* The database fields mirror what the Bank of Greece will ask of us. Our compliance data model in Databricks should produce entity-level ML/TF risk data without manual collation.

**PSD3/PSR: Official Journal publication expected Dec 2026 – Jan 2027** (28 Oct). The EBA expects the PSR to extend name-IBAN verification to credit transfers in all EU currencies. Application follows roughly 18 months after entry into force. ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/verification-of-payee-one-year-instant-payments-2026))

*Why it matters for us:* This sets the PSD3 programme clock to mid-2028. The open-banking permission dashboard and fraud-liability changes should be scoped in this budget cycle.

**AI Act: next hard date is 2 December 2026** (Upcoming). The Digital Omnibus, in force since 27 July, moved Annex III high-risk duties (including credit scoring) to 2 Dec 2027. The Article 50(2) machine-readable marking duty for generative systems already on the market before August 2026 still applies from 2 Dec 2026. ([Usercentrics summary](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/))

*Why it matters for us:* We need a list of customer-facing GenAI outputs (chatbots, generated letters) and a marking approach for them within four weeks.

**EBA advises on simpler Taxonomy disclosures** (29 Oct). The EBA published advice on targeted simplifications to Taxonomy disclosures for banks and investment firms. It affects ESG reporting rather than architecture. ([EBA press releases](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases))

*Why it matters for us:* No architecture action; flag to the ESG reporting data owners in case Databricks reporting pipelines need to shrink.

## 2. Payments

**ECB selects digital euro network-service providers** (30 Oct – 2 Nov). Two consortia will carry traffic between banks, national central banks and the digital euro platform. Nexi with Deutsche Telekom has been live for the pilot since 28 September; Telefónica with Indra's Minsait joins from May 2027 on a ten-year term, bringing MPLS/SD-WAN, API integration, certificates, encryption and a SOC. Both should support a live digital euro from 2029, if the regulation passes and the Governing Council decides to issue. ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/ecb-picks-nexi-and-telefonica-consortia-to-run-digital-euro-network-connections/), [Telefónica / Minsait release](https://news.europawire.eu/telefonica-and-minsait-selected-to-build-secure-digital-euro-communications-infrastructure-for-ecb/eu-press-release/2026/11/02/12/17/10/185523/))

*Why it matters for us:* Connectivity becomes a concrete design choice. Treat it like TIPS access: pick a primary provider, plan for dual-homing, and compare on-boarding through APIM/TIBCO with a dedicated gateway. Add it to the 2027 target architecture now.

**Verification of Payee at one year: it works, but corporates opt out** (28 Oct). The EBA calls the rollout broadly successful. ECB commentary and one Deutsche Bank sample (January 2026: 80% of SCT batch files opted out) show ERP integration lagging, and a single no-match can stall a whole batch. EPC VoP rulebook v1.1 has applied since 20 September; v2.0, with a formal inquiry and compensation process, is due around end-November. ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/verification-of-payee-one-year-instant-payments-2026))

*Why it matters for us:* Check our own opt-out ratio and VoP responder latency. Budget for rulebook 2.0 changes to the inquiry and dispute flows in Appian.

**ISO 20022 structured-address cut-off: 14–15 November** (14–15 Nov). With Swift SR2026, fully unstructured postal addresses are no longer supported in CBPR+. Town and country must at least sit in their own fields for all agents and parties; a BIC alone still works for agents. Swift offers no contingency, and EPC payment messages reject unstructured addresses from 15 November. ([Swift notice](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/iso-20022-milestone-november-2026-unstructured-addresses-be-removed))

*Why it matters for us:* This is a data-quality problem in the IBM i customer master as much as a TIBCO mapping problem. Run a reject simulation on last month's outbound traffic this week. Swift's page also mentions an August notice on extending the migration, so confirm scope with the Swift relationship team.

No material Wero/EPI, TARGET2/T2 or TIPS releases in the window.

## 3. Greece

**DIAS is a founding member of a European council on agentic payments** (22 Oct). PPRO, BLIK, EPI (Wero), Bancontact and DIAS formed the Local Payments Council for Agentic Commerce. It will set common standards for out-of-band authorisation, agent identity on non-card rails, mandates and delegated consent, and disputes and liability. ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/ppro-launches-local-payments-council-for-agentic-commerce), [BlackFin Tech Weekly](https://blackfintech.substack.com/p/blackfin-tech-weekly-november-2nd))

*Why it matters for us:* IRIS is likely to be the first Greek rail AI agents can call. Ask DIAS which working groups are open to member banks, so we shape the consent and liability model instead of inheriting it.

**Fintech Athens 5.0 puts agentic commerce on the Greek agenda** (1 Nov). NBG co-organised the event. NBG Pay's CEO argued that acquirers are moving from processing to merchant growth services, and that consumers must keep control of their own AI assistants. ([Greek City Times](https://greekcitytimes.com/2026/11/01/banks-prepare-for-ai-driven-shopping-and-payments))

*Why it matters for us:* Peer banks are framing agentic commerce as a merchant-services play; our position paper on agent access should cover the acquiring side too.

**Results season: Piraeus 9M, NBG on 4 Nov** (30 Oct). Piraeus reported 9M 2026 on 30 October: core operating profit €930m, cost-to-income 33%, client deposits €69.2bn. The release had no technology disclosures. NBG reports on 4 November. ([Piraeus 9M results](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/piraeus-bank-nine-month-2026-104300090.html), [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/11/03/national-bank-of-greece-sets-date-for-third-quarter-results))

*Why it matters for us:* Cost-to-income near 33% keeps pressure on run-cost; expect the CTO to ask how the 2027 roadmap moves that number.

No Bank of Greece regulatory publications of note in the window.

## 4. Payment & banking security

**Binance hot-wallet breach** (29 Oct). Assets worth up to about $5.8bn moved from several Binance hot wallets to attacker-labelled addresses. Tether and Circle froze more than $2.3bn of stablecoins. Binance pointed to a compromised engineer laptop and is still investigating; a reported FBI link to North Korea is not confirmed in primary sources. ([BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/binance-probes-potential-58-billion-crypto-theft-hack))

*Why it matters for us:* The likely pattern is a privileged-endpoint compromise feeding automated signing. Review controls on our high-value signing paths: HSM-backed payment release, TIBCO admin consoles, AKS cluster-admin, and break-glass access to IBM i.

**Google Cloud outage follow-up: EBA queries a critical provider** (20–21 Oct). A power event in europe-west2 (London) on 20 October cut Revolut card payments and disrupted Lloyds and HSBC for hours. The EBA contacted Google Cloud as a DORA-designated critical provider and stressed that designation does not move incident responsibility away from banks. Google's post-incident analysis is pending. ([The Star / Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/21/europe039s-financial-regulators-query-google-over-cloud-outage-that-hit-banks))

*Why it matters for us:* Supervisors will ask every bank whether single-region dependencies are tested. Our Azure region-pair failover for AKS and the Kafka replication topology should be ready to show evidence.

**Exploitation and vulnerabilities (reported)** (26–29 Oct). CISA confirmed in-the-wild exploitation of the PHP2Shell bugs, seen chained with a Palo Alto Networks firewall flaw. Red Hat issued a critical Linux bulletin (RHSB-2026-016) with patches pending at the time of reporting. Three South Korean financial firms, including Bithumb, disclosed breaches. ([This Week in Security](https://this.weekinsecurity.com/this-week-in-security-november-1-2026-edition))

*Why it matters for us:* Check RHEL/OpenShift exposure on on-prem Kubernetes nodes against RHSB-2026-016, confirm the perimeter firewall estate is patched, and report status to the CISO.

**KubeCon NA next week (9–12 Nov): agent security as a platform problem** (27–29 Oct). CNCF's pre-event writing argues an agent's blast radius is whatever its tools permit. Volcano 1.16 added namespace-level hierarchical queues for multi-tenant batch and AI scheduling. ([CNCF blog](https://www.cncf.io/blog/), [CNCF announcements](https://cncf.io/news/announcements))

*Why it matters for us:* Agent tool scopes belong in the platform layer (AKS policies, workload identity, APIM), not in each team's prompt code. Worth standardising as a pattern.

## 6. Enterprise AI in banking

**HKMA launches Project SAGE on AI-agent identity** (2 Nov). The discussion paper proposes Know Your Agent standards: signed identity certificates, capability disclosures, behavioural audit logs, and a link from each agent action to a licensed principal. It also floats a registry of material agents and powers to suspend individual agent IDs. It builds on the FSB's 12 AI sound practices from mid-October. ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329929/20261103/hong-kong-becomes-first-banking-regulator-require-identity-credentials-ai-agents.htm), [FSB sound practices](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/10/fsb-issues-sound-practices-for-responsible-adoption-of-ai/))

*Why it matters for us:* The clearest supervisory blueprint yet for agent governance. It maps onto Entra workload identities, APIM policies and an agent inventory in our model-risk register; design to it ahead of any EBA equivalent.

**US banks block third-party AI agents using customer credentials** (27–28 Oct). JPMorgan and Bank of America are detecting and blocking agents that log in with customers' usernames and passwords, and are steering toward read-only access. Bank of America told customers they are responsible for activity done with shared credentials. ([Malwarebytes](https://malwarebytes.com/blog/ai/2026/10/banks-are-blocking-ai-agents-from-customer-accounts))

*Why it matters for us:* PSD2/PSD3 already route third-party access through APIs, but agentic browsers bring screen-scraping back. We need agent detection on e-banking and a position on scoped, delegated OAuth consent for agents.
