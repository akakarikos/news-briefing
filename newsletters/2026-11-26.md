## 1. European regulation & supervision

**EBA report: stablecoins, including MiCA e-money tokens, are central to DeFi** (25 Nov). The EBA finds that stablecoins, including some MiCA e-money tokens, provide most of DeFi's liquidity, collateral and settlement. It warns that this can pass risk back to issuers and to the CASPs that act as gateways, and that DeFi structures can be used to get around MiCA's ban on paying interest on EMTs. The EBA again asks the Commission to consider extra rules for CASPs offering DeFi lending access, as part of the MiCA review. ([EBA press release, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-examines-use-stablecoins-including-emts-decentralised-finance))

*Why it matters for us:* If we issue, distribute or custody EMTs, expect DeFi-exposure controls to come out of the MiCA review. Wallet and transaction monitoring should be able to flag flows to DeFi protocols.

There were no new DORA, PSD3/PSR, FiDA, AMLA or AI Act texts. The EBA issued a hotfix for reporting framework v4.4 Phase 1 (25 Nov), which our regulatory-reporting team should apply.

## 2. Payments

**ECB: DLT-issued assets can be pledged as Eurosystem collateral from June 2027** (23 Nov). Assets issued on DLT platforms licensed under the DLT Pilot Regime become eligible collateral if they reach T2S through an eligible CSD link and meet extra conditions on settlement finality and outsourcing. Detailed requirements are due in spring 2027, with go-live in June 2027. The ECB presents this as a step towards a tokenised collateral framework. ([ECB press release, 23 Nov 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261123~76b6f57b74.en.html))

*Why it matters for us:* ECMS connectivity, the securities master on IBM i and the custody feeds will need to handle DLT-issued ISINs. Put tokenised collateral on the 2027 target architecture and ask Treasury about Pilot Regime issuances in its pipeline.

**ECB SPACE 2026: cards overtake cash at the point of sale for the first time** (25 Nov). Cards made up 45% of in-store transactions by number, against 44% for cash, the first time cards have led since the series began in 2016. Mobile payments reached 9%, up from 6%. In every euro-area country at least 12% of consumers had a card or mobile payment refused in the previous month. The release gives no Greek figures. ([ECB press release, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261125~26d1ade228.en.html))

*Why it matters for us:* This supports investment in mobile wallets, IRIS and SoftPOS, and in capacity for card authorisation. Ask the payments analysts to pull the Greek annex from the full report.

**Sweden signs up to join T2 for krona large-value payments, with migration targeted for February 2030** (25 Nov). The ECB and the Riksbank signed an agreement to move Swedish krona RTGS settlement to T2, with migration targeted for February 2030. T2 already settles Danish krone, and Sweden already uses TIPS. ([ECB press release, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261125_1~6333a90f3a.en.html))

*Why it matters for us:* Nothing changes for us directly. It strengthens the case for building our payment hub's RTGS and instant adapters on Eurosystem ISO 20022 standards rather than national variants.

There was no digital euro trilogue news and no TIPS, SEPA Inst, Wero or EPC releases in the window. Swift's new CBPR+ timeline for structured addresses is still due by December.

## 4. Payment & banking security

**Catch-up: Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938), a pre-auth heap corruption in the HotSpot JVM's Base64 decoder** (17 Nov). CVE-2026-114938 is a heap out-of-bounds write in the HotSpot JVM's x86-64 Base64 intrinsic. It can be reached on AVX-512 VBMI CPUs, which covers most current server generations, and researchers showed pre-auth RCE in Jira, Jenkins and Keycloak. Java 17 (from 17.0.6), 21, 25 and 27 are affected, while Java 8 and 11 are not listed. CVSS 4.0 is 9.2. Oracle and OpenJDK patched on 17 Nov and CISA urged immediate patching. There was no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation as of 18 Nov, but opportunistic probing has been reported. The stopgap is to restart JVMs with -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics. ([BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/critical-java4shell-flaw-allows-rce-attacks-on-major-java-applications))

*Why it matters for us:* Our exposure is TIBCO BW/EMS, Kafka brokers and Connect, Keycloak, Jenkins and Spring on AKS, mostly running on AVX-512 Azure SKUs. Rebuild base images and use the flag where patching has to wait. IBM i Java runs on J9 rather than HotSpot, so it is probably out of scope, but get confirmation from IBM. Collect TIBCO, Confluent and Databricks bulletins as DORA evidence.

The n0n vendor breach (issues 3 and 5) produced no new confirmed facts. One threat-research write-up blames compromised credentials at the analytics vendor Qubole, which the banks have not confirmed.

## 8. From InfoQ

### Architecture & Design

**CNCF research finds VMs still host 53% of production apps** (24 Nov). A CNCF and Linux Foundation survey of 492 organisations finds that VMs host 53% of production apps on average, and 64% at large enterprises. 71% expect that share to stay flat. Where VM use is growing, the reasons are isolation (47%) and AI or agent workloads (34%). MicroVM sandboxing and confidential computing are the expected growth areas. ([InfoQ, 24 Nov 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/cncf-vm-ai-workloads/))

*Why it matters for us:* This supports a hybrid AKS, VM and IBM i platform strategy rather than a containers-only target.

**DoorDash splits coordination from computation and scales dispatch 10x** (25 Nov). DoorDash split dispatch into a stateful coordinator that owns lifecycle, state and rules, and stateless workers that run optimisation and inference on sharded data over gRPC. The result was 10x throughput and availability above 99.99% at peak. ([InfoQ, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/doordash-dispatch-redesign/))

*Why it matters for us:* The same split suits fraud scoring and payment routing: one owner for state, stateless scoring scaled out on AKS, and a clean failure boundary for DORA scenario tests.

### AI architecture

**evals, grounding and release gates for AI-drafted suspicious activity reports** (24 Nov). A reference design for drafting suspicious activity reports with a multi-agent pipeline. Every claim is grounded with a source ID, and a judge model plus a deterministic fact-checker gate publication. A golden dataset gates every prompt or model change, and releases need compliance, risk and legal sign-off before shadow and canary rollout. When evidence is missing the agent returns 'insufficient context' instead of guessing. ([InfoQ article, 24 Nov 2026](https://www.infoq.com/articles/llm-agent-architecture-evals-grounding/))

*Why it matters for us:* This is close to a ready-made template for our financial-crime GenAI pilot on Azure OpenAI and Databricks. Treat prompts and models as regulated changes under model risk management, and freeze the judge model's version.

**securing AI agents as an insider-risk problem (QCon SF)** (23 Nov). Google's Amanda Walker argues that agents need their own principals, because an agent holding a user's authority can be turned against that user through prompt injection. She recommends attenuated tokens minted per sub-agent, Biscuit-style tokens that can only be narrowed, and an egress proxy that swaps short-lived synthetic keys for real ones. Containment comes from sandboxes and sidecar policy over what an agent can call. ([InfoQ, 23 Nov 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/qcon-ai-agent-security/))

*Why it matters for us:* This fits Entra Agent ID and the AKS Agent Sandbox (issue 4). Make agent principals, attenuated tokens and an APIM egress proxy mandatory in our agent harness standard.

## 9. Top 3 from Medium

**Dual Writes Will Eventually Corrupt Your Data: How We Migrated to the Transactional Outbox Pattern — Sonam Patel (tag: kafka)** (25 Nov). A production write-up that starts from 1,284 orders sitting in Postgres that no downstream service ever received, the classic dual-write failure. It describes the migration to a transactional outbox (summary from the feed preview). ([Medium — Sonam Patel](https://medium.com/@patelsonam097/dual-writes-will-eventually-corrupt-your-data-how-we-migrated-to-the-transactional-outbox-pattern-6fe87c96d772))

*Why it matters for us:* We carry the same risk wherever an AKS service or TIBCO process writes to a database and publishes to Kafka in two steps. Outbox plus CDC should be the default in our event-driven integration standard.

**Your Azure MCP is only as safe as the identity it runs with — Part 1 — Camilla Santoro (tag: azure)** (25 Nov). Many teams run the Azure MCP Server under their own admin identity, so every misunderstood prompt runs with admin rights. Part 1 argues for a dedicated, least-privilege identity (summary from the feed preview). ([Medium — Camilla Santoro](https://medium.com/@camilla.santoro124/your-azure-mcp-is-only-as-safe-as-the-identity-it-runs-with-part-1-042bd0031aad))

*Why it matters for us:* Require managed identities with scoped RBAC and read-only defaults before the platform team adopts Azure MCP.

**Why FAPI 2.0 and DPoP Are the Future of Corporate Open Banking — Davide V. (tag: open-banking)** (24 Nov). The article argues for moving B2B banking API security from mTLS to application-layer cryptography under FAPI 2.0, using private_key_jwt client authentication (RFC 7523) and DPoP sender-constrained tokens (RFC 9449) (summary from the feed preview). ([Medium — Davide V.](https://medium.com/@davide.vitali/why-fapi-2-0-and-dpop-are-the-future-of-corporate-open-banking-08b5f657452a))

*Why it matters for us:* This matters for the APIM roadmap for corporate and PSD3/PSR APIs. DPoP survives proxies and gateways that break mTLS binding, so assess FAPI 2.0 support in APIM and our identity provider.
